# Long-term investing (Canada)

Low-fee, diversified, boring, and automatic. Data: `data/investing.toml`.

> **This is education, not personal advice. The assistant is not a licensed advisor.**

Last checked: 2026-10-18

## Order of operations

1. Emergency fund: 3–6 months of expenses in a low-fee account.
2. Pay off high-interest debt (credit cards first).
3. Take any employer match.
4. FHSA if you are a first-time home buyer.
5. RRSP when your marginal rate is high.
6. TFSA for everything else and for money you may need sooner.
7. Non-registered once registered room is full.

## A low-fee core

- One all-in-one ETF is enough for most people:
  - All stocks: XEQT, VEQT, ZEQT (MER about 0.19–0.22%).
  - 80/20: XGRO, VGRO. 60/40: XBAL, VBAL.
- Pick the stock/bond mix you can hold through a 30–50% drop without selling.
- Fees compound. A 2% mutual fund costs about 10x a 0.2% ETF.

## Asset location

- **RRSP**: US-listed US ETFs (VTI, SCHD, VYM). The tax treaty removes the 15% US dividend withholding there.
- **RRSP first for bonds**: interest is taxed at your full rate.
- **Non-registered**: Canadian stocks (XIC, VDY). The dividend tax credit only works here.
- **TFSA**: your highest-growth assets.
- Simple beats perfect. Holding one all-in-one ETF in every account is fine.

## Rebalancing

- All-in-one ETFs rebalance themselves.
- With several funds, rebalance once a year or when a part drifts more than 5 points from target.
- Rebalance with new money first. That avoids selling and taxes.

## Cash parking

- Short-term money: high-interest savings, CASH (HISA ETF) or ZMMK (money market). Not CDIC-insured when held as an ETF.

## Behaviour

- Automate contributions on payday.
- Do not time the market. Time in the market matters more.
- Do not check daily. Review once or twice a year.
