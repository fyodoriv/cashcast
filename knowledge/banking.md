# Banking: fees, minimum balances and rebates

Should you pay a monthly fee or park a minimum balance? Data: `data/banks.toml`.

Last checked: 2026-10-18

## The break-even rule

- A minimum balance is not free. That money could earn interest or investment returns elsewhere.
- **Break-even return = annual fee ÷ minimum balance.**
- If you can earn **more** than that elsewhere, pay the fee (or switch banks).
- If you earn **less**, keep the minimum balance.

## Worked examples

| Account | Annual fee | Minimum | Break-even |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scotiabank Ultimate Package | 371.40 | 6,000 | 6.2% |
| TD All-Inclusive | 371.40 | 6,000 | 6.2% |
| TD Unlimited | 215.40 | 4,000 | 5.4% |
| Scotiabank Preferred Package | 203.40 | 4,000 | 5.1% |
| CIBC Smart Account (tier 1) | 203.40 | 4,000 | 5.1% |
| TD Every Day | 143.40 | 3,000 | 4.8% |

- High-interest savings pays well below these break-evens in 2026, so holding the minimum usually wins **if you have the cash anyway**.

## Watch the rule

- Most big banks need the balance **every day of the month**. One dip for one day costs the full monthly fee.
- Keep a buffer above the minimum.
- CIBC's higher tiers use your **average monthly** balance across deposits and investments.

## Add the card rebate

- Some packages rebate a credit-card annual fee:
  - Scotiabank Ultimate: up to 150 per year (Passport, Momentum and others).
  - Scotiabank Preferred: 40, only after 15,000 of card spend; not the first year.
  - TD All-Inclusive: up to 139 every year (TD Cash Back Visa Infinite and others).
  - TD Unlimited: up to 139, **first year only**.
  - CIBC Smart tier 2: up to 50; tier 3: up to 139.
  - RBC VIP: full fee on several RBC premium cards.
- Subtract the rebate from the fee before you compute break-even.

## No-fee options

- Wealthsimple chequing: no fees; 1.25% (Core), 1.75% (Premium, 100,000+ assets), 2.25% (Generation, 500,000+). Plus 0.5% with 2,000+ direct deposit for Core and Premium.
- EQ Bank, Tangerine, Simplii: no monthly fees.
- Trade-off: fewer branches, and no card-fee rebates from big banks.
