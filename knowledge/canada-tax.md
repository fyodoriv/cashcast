# Canadian personal tax basics (2026)

Plain-English guide. Numbers live in `data/tax_ca_2026.toml` and `data/tax_deductions.toml`.
This is education, not tax advice.

Last checked: 2026-10-18

## Brackets: how they work

- Tax rates rise in steps. Each rate applies only to the income **inside** that step.
- A raise never lowers your take-home pay.
- You pay federal tax plus provincial or territorial tax.
- 2026 federal rates: 14% to 58,523; 20.5% to 117,045; 26% to 181,440; 29% to 258,482; 33% above.
- Your **marginal rate** is the combined rate on your next dollar. Use it to judge RRSP deductions.

## RRSP vs TFSA

- **RRSP**: deduct now, pay tax when you withdraw.
  - Best when your marginal rate today is **higher** than your expected rate in retirement.
  - 2026 limit: 18% of 2025 earned income, max 33,810, plus unused room.
  - Deadline for 2026 deductions: March 1, 2027.
- **TFSA**: no deduction, but growth and withdrawals are tax-free.
  - Best when your income is low or middle, or when you may need the money sooner.
  - 2026 room: 7,000 plus unused room. Withdrawals come back as room next year.
- Similar rates now and later? The two give about the same result. The TFSA is more flexible.

## FHSA (First Home Savings Account)

- For first-time buyers (no home you owned and lived in this year or the past 4 years).
- 8,000 per year, 40,000 lifetime. Carry forward up to 8,000 of unused room.
- Deductible like an RRSP and tax-free out for a qualifying home. Best of both.
- Not used for a home? Move it to your RRSP tax-free, without using RRSP room.

## HBP (Home Buyers' Plan)

- Withdraw up to **60,000** from your RRSP for a first home.
- Repay over 15 years. Missed repayments become taxable income.
- You can use the FHSA and the HBP for the same home.

## Capital gains

- Only **50%** of a capital gain is taxed (the inclusion rate).
- The proposed 2/3 rate was cancelled in March 2025. 50% is the law for 2026.
- Capital losses offset capital gains. Unused losses carry back 3 years or forward forever.

## ACB (adjusted cost base)

- ACB is your average cost per share, including commissions.
- For identical shares (same stock or ETF), **average** the cost across all buys.
- Track ACB for non-registered accounts. Brokers often get it wrong after transfers.

## Superficial loss (30-day rule)

- You cannot claim a loss if you, your spouse, or a company you control buy the same or identical property **30 days before or after** the sale, and still hold it on day 30.
- The denied loss is added to the ACB of the new shares, so it is delayed, not lost.
- Tax-loss harvesting: switch to a similar but not identical ETF (for example XEQT to VEQT).

## Crypto: capital or business income

- Most investors report crypto gains as **capital gains** (50% taxable).
- Frequent trading, short holds, or running it like a business can make it **business income** (100% taxable).
- Crypto-to-crypto trades are taxable dispositions. Convert each to CAD.

## T1135 foreign property

- File Form T1135 if the total **cost** of specified foreign property is over **100,000 CAD** at any time in the year.
- Includes US-listed stocks held outside RRSPs and TFSAs, and foreign bank accounts.
- Canadian-listed ETFs that hold US stocks do not count.
- Penalties for late filing are steep, even when no tax is owed.

## GST/HST for sole proprietors

- You must register once taxable sales pass **30,000** in one calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive quarters (small-supplier limit).
- Below that, registration is optional. Registering lets you claim input tax credits.
- Charge the rate of the customer's province.

## CPP and EI for the self-employed

- You pay **both halves** of CPP: 11.9% on earnings between 3,500 and 74,600, plus 8% CPP2 up to 85,000.
- Half of CPP is deductible; the other half is a credit.
- EI is optional for the self-employed. Opting in gives special benefits (for example sickness), after a 12-month wait.

## Instalments

- Pay quarterly instalments if your net tax owing is over **3,000** (1,800 in Quebec) this year **and** in either of the two prior years.
- Due dates: March 15, June 15, September 15, December 15.
- Common triggers: self-employment, large investment income, RSUs with low withholding.

## Filing dates

- Most people: file and pay by **April 30**.
- Self-employed: file by **June 15**, but still pay by April 30.
