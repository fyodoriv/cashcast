# Insurance basics (Canada)

Rules of thumb and gap checks. Data: `data/insurance.toml`.
This is education, not advice. A licensed advisor or broker sets real coverage.

Last checked: 2026-10-18

## Order of priority

1. **Liability** (home or tenant, auto). One lawsuit can cost more than you own.
2. **Disability**. Losing your income is the biggest risk in working years.
3. **Term life**, if anyone depends on your income or shares your debts.
4. **Travel medical** before every trip outside your province.
5. Optional: critical illness, umbrella.

## Term life

- Need = years of support x after-tax income + debts + final costs + goals − savings and existing cover.
- Shortcut for parents of young children: 7–10x gross income.
- No dependants and no shared debt: you may need little or none.
- Match the term to the need (until the kids are independent or the mortgage is paid).
- Group life at work is often 1–2x salary and ends when you leave.

## Disability

- Aim to replace 60–70% of gross income. FCAC says plans generally replace 60–85%.
- Check group long-term disability at work first.
- Group benefits paid by the employer are taxable. Benefits from a policy you pay yourself are tax-free.
- Self-employed: no group plan and no EI sickness unless you opt in to EI special benefits.

## Tenant and home

- **Renters**: get tenant insurance. It covers your belongings, liability (standard 1 million, often raisable to 2 million) and extra living costs after a loss. Many leases require it.
- **Owners**: insure the full rebuild cost, not the market value.
- Add sewer-backup and overland-water cover where you can. Water damage is often excluded by default.

## Auto

- Carry liability well above the provincial minimum (1–2 million).
- Drop collision on an old car when 2–3 years of premium is more than the car is worth.
- Rules differ by province. BC, SK, MB and QC have public insurers for base cover.

## Travel medical

- Provincial health plans pay little or nothing abroad.
- Card cover has limits on trip length and age. Examples:
  - Scotia Momentum Visa Infinite: 15 days, under 65.
  - TD Cash Back Visa Infinite: 10 days (4 days at 65+).
  - MBNA Rewards World Elite: 21 days, under 65.
  - National Bank World Elite: 60 days to age 54, then shorter.
  - Desjardins Cash Back World Elite: only 3-day trips.
- Longer trip or older traveller? Buy a top-up or an annual plan.
- Trip cancellation and delay cover usually needs 75–100% of the fare on the card.

## Other

- **Critical illness**: optional; lump sum on diagnosis. Lower priority than disability.
- **Umbrella**: 1–2 million extra liability if you have high net worth, rentals, a pool, or a teen driver.
- **Mobile device**: many premium cards cover phones bought or financed on the card. Skip the carrier plan.
- **Creditor insurance** from a lender is usually worse value than personal term life.

## Gap checks the assistant runs

- Paying rent but no tenant-insurance premium.
- Paying a mortgage but no home-insurance premium.
- Flights or hotels booked, but no card with travel medical and no travel policy.
- Travel charged to a card without cover while another card has it.
- Dependants listed but no life insurance.
- Self-employed income but no disability premium.
- Car costs but no auto premium.
- Car rental on a card without rental-car cover.
